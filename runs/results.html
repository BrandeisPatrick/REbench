rebuild-bench · calc task · 12 rebuilds by 4 models over 3 rounds · test sets sealed1 and rust1

Every model built a working engine within 20 minutes. The rest of the hour set them apart.

Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1 and Haiku 5.5 each rebuilt LibreOffice Calc's formula engine from scratch three times, with 60 minutes per attempt and no internet. The last round required Rust. Every engine was graded on about 9,500 hidden formula cells and timed against LibreOffice on four workloads. Across the three attempts, Opus's median engine runs 27 times faster than LibreOffice, Sonnet's 17, Fable's 11 and Haiku's 1.2. This page explains where those differences come from and what they mean for the benchmark.

Opus beats Sonnet in 7 of 9 pairings of their attempts, Sonnet beats Fable in 7 of 9, and Opus beats Fable in 8 of 9. Every model beats Haiku in all 9. On sealed1, the other test set, the medians are 27.0x, 18.2x, 10.6x and 0.90x, in the same order.

Single attempts overlap. Sonnet's best attempt (20.0x) beat Opus's worst (16.8x), and Fable's best (17.7x) beat Sonnet's worst (12.3x). Depending on which single attempts you pick, the top three can come out in any of the six possible orders. Cost follows yet another order: Fable's logged runs cost about $29 and $44 at API prices, Opus's $20 and $26, Sonnet's $11 and $12, and Haiku's about $3 each.

Every engine passed the public check within 18 minutes

The models could run the grader's own check on 40 public workbooks at any time. In the eight runs with full logs, every engine first passed that check between minute 7 and minute 18. At that point its own speed check read between 1.6x and 9.7x. What happened next depended on the model.

The remaining errors are LibreOffice conventions, and most engines miss the same ones

All twelve engines got between 99.85% and 100% of the hidden cells right on both test sets. Across the 24 gradings that leaves 142 wrong cells, and we sorted every one. Only 6 are ordinary bugs. The other 136 come from five rules in areas the task contract tells the models to work out for themselves, such as error propagation and number-to-text conversion.

Speed comes from two data structures more than from the language

Most of the speed score comes from two workloads where LibreOffice is slow: 5,000 exact lookups over 20,000 rows (1.6 s) and 1,000 conditional sums over 50,000 rows (5.2 s). Two classic structures decide them: a hash index for exact-match lookups, and a cache of the rows that match each criterion. We checked every engine's source code for both.

Compiled engines with an index ran lookups 50 to 138 times faster than LibreOffice. The one without, Sonnet's sealed engine, reached 11.1x. With a criteria cache, conditional sums ran 33 to 84 times faster. Fable's sealed Go engine had none and reached 15.4x, even though it spread the work over four cores. Python costs Haiku roughly another factor of ten: its sealed engine had both structures and still ran lookups at 8.4x.

The dependency chain is mostly a file-loading test. LibreOffice spends 110 ms loading it and 21 ms calculating it, and Opus's Rust engine timed itself at about 26 of its 30 ms on unzipping and parsing. It still ranks the models in the same order in every round: Opus, Sonnet, Fable, Haiku.

What to change in the benchmark

  • Run at least three attempts per model and report the median and head-to-head win rates. One attempt can move a model's speed by 1.7x.
  • Test LibreOffice's conventions directly. Build a probe set for each rule (error codes, number printing, empty-cell criteria, coercion, TRUE/FALSE results) and score each one. Keep some rules out of the public examples, so that finding them takes queries to LibreOffice. That measures reverse engineering instead of example-fitting, and it stops the gate from flipping between test sets.
  • Make the speed workloads exercise the engine. Scale them up 10 to 100 times, add recalculation after single-cell edits, and time loading separately, so that file parsing stops dominating.
  • Remove the 8x cap from the score and the contract. Opus, Sonnet and Fable all score the maximum 1.000 whenever they pass. A 64x cap, or plain log speed, would separate 10x from 27x.
  • Measure persistence on its own. Add a condition where the model must keep improving until the hour ends, to see what the 10 to 41 unused minutes are worth.
  • Add a GUI-only condition if computer use is the goal. In twelve runs no model touched the LibreOffice window.

Data

How the benchmark works

Each attempt had to rebuild the formula engine of LibreOffice Calc 25.2 (87 functions) as a program that reads an .xlsx workbook and writes every formula's value. It had 60 minutes in an offline sandbox with LibreOffice installed, 40 public example workbooks with expected values, and a check command that scores the public set with the grader's rules.

The score has a correctness gate (99.9% of hidden cells and 95% of hidden workbooks), then speed against LibreOffice in a warm process (load plus full recalculation) on four workloads, combined as a geometric mean. A workload under 99.9% correct counts as 1/8. The score is 0.5 at parity and 1.0 at 8x.

The pilot ran the models as subagents with a free choice of language. The sealed round ran headless Claude Code with only the sandbox tools, still with a free choice. The Rust round used the sealed harness and required Rust, which the grader enforced. Test set sealed1 was generated after the sealed round and rust1 after the Rust round, with 160 workbooks each. All grades come from an idle machine; regrading two sealed engines in the last pass reproduced their earlier speeds within about 5%.
